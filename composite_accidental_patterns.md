# Composite and Accidental Fingerprint Patterns

This document provides an overview and visual examples of composite and accidental fingerprint patterns, which are crucial in forensic science for individual identification.

## Whorls
- Form circular or spiral patterns, like tiny whirlpools.
- Four groups of whorls: plain (concentric circles), central pocket loop (a loop with a whorl at the end), double loop (two loops that create an S-like pattern) and accidental loop (irregular shaped).
- Whorls make up about 35 percent of pattern types.

## Composites
- Composites are the 4th category of the fingerprint pattern. They are a mixture of Arches, loops, and Whorls. Around 1-2% of the population have these composites.
- Classified into 4 types:

### Central Pocket Loops

**Description:** A combination of a loop and a whorl. Most ridges form a loop, but one or more ridges recurve around the core, forming a pocket. An imaginary line drawn between two deltas should not intersect any of these recurving ridges.

![Central Pocket Loop](/home/ubuntu/flashcard_central_pocket_loop.png)

### Double/Twinned Loops

**Description:** Contains a double-loop pattern. The two loops either overlap or surround each other and contain the point of the core exiting towards different deltas. Sometimes referred to as the "Ying-Yang" symbol.

![Double/Twinned Loops](/home/ubuntu/flashcard_double_twinned_loop.png)

### Lateral Pocket Loops

**Description:** Also a double loop pattern formed by two loops overlapping or surrounding each other. Two different kinds of loops enter from the same end of the finger, recurve, and exit from the same end, with one loop forming a covering-like pocket outside the other loop.

![Lateral Pocket Loop](/home/ubuntu/flashcard_lateral_pocket_loop.png)

### Accidental

**Description:** Formed with the combination of two different fingerprint patterns except for plain arch. They have 2 or more deltas. They are too irregular to be placed under the typical categories of loops, arches, or whorls.
